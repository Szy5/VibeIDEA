# Prior Work Analysis Report

## Target Paper

**Title:** Improving Retrieval Augmented Language Model with Self-Reasoning

**arXiv ID:** [N/A](https://arxiv.org/abs/N/A)

**Abstract:** 
> The Retrieval-Augmented Language Model (RALM) has demonstrated remarkable performance on knowledge-intensive tasks by integrating external knowledge during inference, which mitigates the factual hallucinations inherited in large language models (LLMs). Despite these advancements, challenges persist in the implementation of RALMs, particularly in terms of reliability and traceability. Specifically, the irrelevant document retrieval may result in unhelpful responses or even deteriorate the performance of LLMs, while the lack of appropriate citations in outputs complicates efforts to verify the trustworthiness of the models. To this end, we propose a novel self-reasoning framework aimed at improving the reliability and traceability of RALMs, whose core idea is to leverage reasoning trajectories generated by the LLM itself. The framework involves constructing self-reasoning trajectories through three processes: a relevance-aware process, an evidence-aware selective process, and a trajectory analysis process. We evaluated our framework across four public datasets (two short-form QA datasets, one long-form QA dataset, and one fact verification dataset) to demonstrate its superiority. Our method can outperform existing state-of-the-art models and achieve performance comparable with GPT-4, using only 2,000 training samples.

**Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations** (2023)
- *Authors:* Tianyu Gao et al.
- *Direct Connection:* This work provided a practical framework and metrics (citation precision/recall, EM recall) for citation-grounded generation and an automatic citation evaluator leveraged for data quality control, which this paper builds upon and augments with structured evidence selection and trajectory analysis.

### 🏷️ Inspiration

**ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models** (2023) [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)]
- *Authors:* Shunyu Yao et al.
- *Direct Connection:* By interleaving chain-of-thought with retrieval actions, ReAct inspired the use of explicit, stepwise reasoning that interacts with evidence, which this work formalizes into structured self-generated trajectories for relevance judgment, evidence selection, and final analysis.

**Teaching language models to support answers with verified quotes** (2022) [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11147)]
- *Authors:* Jacob Menick et al.
- *Direct Connection:* By training LMs to ground answers with verifiable quotes/citations, this paper established explicit citation as a core mechanism for trustworthiness that is operationalized here via snippet-level citation and justification in the evidence-aware selective process.

### 🏷️ Gap Identification

**Making Retrieval-Augmented Language Models Robust to Irrelevant Context** (2023) [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01558)]
- *Authors:* Or Honovich (Yoran) et al.
- *Direct Connection:* This work pinpointed RAG’s brittleness to irrelevant retrieval and proposed external NLI-based filtering, a dependency and cost the current paper removes by learning internal relevance judgments (RAP) and evidence selection (EAP) without auxiliary models.

### 🏷️ Baseline

**Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection** (2024) [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511)]
- *Authors:* Akari Asai et al.
- *Direct Connection:* As the most similar prior system, Self-RAG showed that self-reflection improves RAG but requires additional critic/generator models and reflection tokens, directly motivating the paper’s end-to-end in-model self-reasoning trajectories (RAP/EAP/TAP) that avoid extra modules and large auxiliary datasets.

### 🏷️ Related Problem

**Interleaving Retrieval with Chain-of-Thought Reasoning for Knowledge-Intensive Multi-Step Questions** (2023) [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10509)]
- *Authors:* Harsh Trivedi et al.
- *Direct Connection:* IRCoT demonstrated that retrieval-guided chain-of-thought boosts multi-step QA, informing the design of retrieval-aware reasoning steps while highlighting susceptibility to error cascades from irrelevant documents that the relevance-aware process is designed to mitigate.

## Synthesis: How Prior Work Led to This Paper

Retrieval-augmented generation framed the central paradigm for knowledge-intensive QA by conditioning language models on external documents, establishing the setting in which improvements to reliability and traceability must operate. Building on that paradigm, ReAct introduced interleaved chain-of-thought with retrieval actions to make reasoning steps explicit and evidence-driven, while IRCoT showed that iterative retrieval guided by reasoning can improve multi-step questions. However, both lines revealed a susceptibility to compounding errors when retrieval is noisy. In parallel, Menick et al. demonstrated that grounding answers with verified quotes can improve trustworthiness, and Gao et al. provided a practical pipeline and metrics for citation-grounded generation, including automatic evaluators for citation precision/recall and EM recall. Addressing robustness, Yoran et al. highlighted that irrelevant context degrades RAG and proposed NLI-based filters—effective but reliant on external tools. Most closely related, Self-RAG showed that self-reflection can improve RAG by learning when to retrieve and how to critique, but it required auxiliary critic/generator models and substantial extra data to predict reflection tokens.
Together, these works expose a gap: RAG needs to be robust to noisy retrieval and verifiable via citations, yet avoid brittle dependencies on external modules or multiple learned agents. The present work synthesizes these insights by internalizing reasoning as self-generated trajectories: first judging document relevance, then selecting and justifying snippet-level evidence with explicit citations, and finally analyzing trajectories to produce answers, all trained end-to-end. It borrows the verifiability ethos and metrics from citation-grounded generation, the structure of retrieval-aware reasoning from ReAct/IRCoT, and addresses the robustness gap identified by Yoran et al., while simplifying Self-RAG’s multi-model setup into a single-model framework.
