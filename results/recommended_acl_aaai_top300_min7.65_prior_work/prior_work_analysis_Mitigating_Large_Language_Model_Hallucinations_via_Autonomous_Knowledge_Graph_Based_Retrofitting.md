## Target Paper

**Title:** Mitigating Large Language Model Hallucinations via Autonomous Knowledge Graph-Based Retrofitting

**arXiv ID:** [N/A](https://arxiv.org/abs/N/A)

**Abstract:** 
> Incorporating factual knowledge in knowledge graph is regarded as a promising approach for mitigating the hallucination of large language models (LLMs). Existing methods usually only use the user's input to query the knowledge graph, thus failing to address the factual hallucination generated by LLMs during its reasoning process. To address this problem, this paper proposes Knowledge Graph-based Retrofitting (KGR), a new framework that incorporates LLMs with KGs to mitigate factual hallucination during the reasoning process by retrofitting the initial draft responses of LLMs based on the factual knowledge stored in KGs. Specifically, KGR leverages LLMs to extract, select, validate, and retrofit factual statements within the model-generated responses, which enables an autonomous knowledge verifying and refining procedure without any additional manual efforts. Experiments show that KGR can significantly improve the performance of LLMs on factual QA benchmarks especially when involving complex reasoning processes, which demonstrates the necessity and effectiveness of KGR in mitigating hallucination and enhancing the reliability of LLMs.

- *Direct Connection:* CRITIC’s tool-interactive, iterative self-critique pattern informed KGR’s design of an LLM-driven pipeline that verifies and revises claims iteratively, with KGR replacing web tools by KG-backed verification and employing few-shot prompting for controlled edits.

- *Direct Connection:* KAPING retrieves KG facts conditioned on the user’s question and feeds them before generation, whose limitation—only question-relevant retrieval—explicitly motivated KGR to instead extract claims from the model’s draft and verify those (often unmentioned) reasoning facts against the KG.

- *Authors:* Zhengbao Ji et al.
- *Direct Connection:* RHO showed KG grounding can reduce hallucinations but focuses on grounding responses with question/topic-relevant knowledge, highlighting the gap that KGR targets by verifying and correcting intermediate claims arising during the model’s reasoning.

## Synthesis: How Prior Work Led to This Paper

Post-generation revision has emerged as a promising way to mitigate hallucinations, with RARR demonstrating that language models can research and revise their own outputs using retrieved evidence from the web, and CRITIC showing that tool-interactive, iterative critiquing enables self-correction through structured prompts. In parallel, KG-based augmentation methods such as KAPING have shown that injecting question-relevant triples from a knowledge graph can improve zero-shot KGQA, while RHO reduces hallucinations by grounding dialogue in external knowledge bases; however, these KG approaches focus on retrieving facts tied to user inputs rather than the often-unmentioned intermediate facts a model invokes during reasoning. StructGPT further established that LLMs can reason over structured inputs like graphs and tables via prompting, indicating that KGs offer precise, structured evidence amenable to LLM consumption. Complementing these directions, in-context editing work by Zheng et al. demonstrated that LLMs can be guided via few-shot prompts to make targeted factual edits, suggesting a viable mechanism for controlled retrofitting.
Collectively, these works surfaced a gap: question-conditioned retrieval and pre-generation conditioning miss hallucinations that arise inside multi-step reasoning, and web-based revision introduces noise and inconsistency. The natural next step was to fuse the revise-after-generation paradigm with structured, high-precision knowledge sources. KGR synthesizes these insights by extracting atomic claims from drafts, mapping them to KG entities, selecting manageable subsets of triples under context limits, verifying each claim against KG evidence, and retrofitting responses iteratively via in-context edits—thereby extending self-correction frameworks with KG-grounded, claim-level verification that addresses hallucinations throughout the reasoning process.
